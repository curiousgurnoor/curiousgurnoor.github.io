We covered 3 main asset classes for which tokenization applies and has seen noticeable activity: Currencies, Equities, & Structured Products.

## 1 Tokenized Currencies
**Tokenized Currencies** are best represented through stablecoins, and with the GENIUS Act treating all stablecoins as equal, the real benefits of one stablecoin as compared to another stem from:

- Depth of Liquidity

- Extent of Adoption (as a base asset)

- Reputation & Trust in the Issuer

Given the regulated denominations of an issued stablecoin, there is essentially a risk-free rate up for grabs for anyone that issues their own stablecoin. This is stipulated on the fact that the platform has enough leverage to promote adoption of their own stablecoin. While the incentive for platforms is clear, the incentives for users to adopt numerous stablecoins is less so. Why would they want to face the choice exhaustion of switching to new stablecoins every time they use a new platform?

This is where zones of interoperability, efficient issuer architecture, and atomic swaps become very important. Similar to how composability and interoperability between Ethereum L2s in the Optimism, ZKSync, or Polygon spheres is very efficient, issuers can exercise similar power amongst their whitelabeled infrastructure. This will allow for easy interoperability and consistently deep liquidity amongst stablecoins issued through a specific platform (ex. M0, Ethena, Bridge, Anchorage, etc.).

We’ve now established the layers of value accrual within the stablecoin stack:

**User :: Platform :: Stablecoin Issuer Infrastructure :: Settlement Chain**

- In most cases, the Settlement Chain has virtually no pricing power here. They benefit from the consistent fees generated by a high velocity of payments (see Tron), and the amplified network effects greater by deeper liquidity existing on the L1.

- The Stablecoin Issuer Infrastrucutre captures marginal fees and benefits from a diverse ecosystem of stablecoin issuers connected to it. While they may demand higher fees at the start, this quickly imitates a SaaS business model, and margins become razor thin (see the Shared Sequencer business model).

- Most of the value rests between the Platform and the User. This split will depend on how unique and how much pricing power the platform holds vs. how easy it is for users to switch onto a competitor.

- _In perfect markets, value is passed back to the user. However, within our imperfect markets, its a negotiation between user and platform._

### 1.1 Stablecoins and International Economic Policy
Stablecoins are this one-way bridge for U.S. government securities and assets abroad to a new investor base, but its not the same for international assets. Everyone wants exposure to USD, and this may exacerbate the political power the U.S. holds as countries continue to denominate currencies in USD, while others ban it to preserve their own economic freedom. It may create a multi-polar set of economic powers with dominant economic policies, or it may create hyper-nationalistic and isolated markets with a strong presence of global unregulated markets.

## 2 Tokenized Equities
**Tokenized Equities** are a strong narrative without any strong fundamentals. The whole process currently operates with a ”trust me, bro” mentality. Tokenized equities are just a representation of the same archaic processes we have off-chain today, but with a fancy receipt of those action onchain. These receipts are either supported by **a hard peg which is susceptible to a depeg** or **a token with no real liquidity, redemption, or liquidation on-chain** . Operating under these assumptions, we are building bad leverage and composability guarantees on top, setting ourselves up for a catastrophic awakening.

We need to wait until the blockchain can become the property rights layer of the Internet, such that tokenized equities issued natively on-chain truly exist in our parallel market. How this happens? Well, I think we begin by tackling the fat long tail of uses cases, aka serving as an after-hours and weekenders market. We then slowly eat up a majority of trading infrastructure. **What are the market mechanisms for calculating prices for RWAs in such a market?** These weekender markets will trade at a premium/discount because of enhanced risk and lack of deep liquidity.

An interesting question I’ve been tackling is ”Has the demand for new assets on-chain become saturated?” and ”What quality do we need in an asset to bring it on-chain? We have stability and risk, so what else do we need? Collateral?”

## 3 Risks in the Tokenization industry
- Tail wagging the dog → the entire RWA industry is propped up on hard-coded oracles, without any sophisticated peg management

- There is a severe lack of onchain redemption, liquidation, and depth of liquidity preferences → price is not price without deep liquidity, tokenization are current wrappers on a trading strategy, where and how sustainable are yields onchain?

## 4 Interesting Takeaways & Phrases
- Blockchains are a manifestation of property rights for the Internet layer.

- DeFi becomes an open unfettered ground for financial innovation, until the primitive becomes powerful enough and retro-fitted for regulatory capture.

- Yield and Risk are two sides of the same coin.

- Crypto transforms discrete areas of risk/yield into continuous surface areas, allowing for complete granularity and freedom in financial engineering. However, capital will continue to concentrate in discrete (but dynamic) ways. Users create the rules and dynamic play within then, as compared to playing in the rule-set provided to them.

- You have to vertically integrate and lock-in the user by offering asset diversity and deep asset liquidity.

- On the risk spectrum, you have safe stablecoins (regulated by the GENIUS Act), tokenized treasuries, and then tokenized yield and structured products.

- Crypto is a global liquidity bridge for introducing American assets to the global market
