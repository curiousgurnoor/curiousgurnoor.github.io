---
title: Solana Seeker Phone Review
---

**PROS:** Lightweight. Solid mid-range Google Android features + Phone with regular phone capabilities with embedded crypto app capabilities and a built-in hardware wallet + embedded wallet with seed phrase, onchain identity, and Proof-of-Humanity.

**CONS:** Setup process for writing down the seed phrase is a high point of friction + Mediocre asset diversity and trading UI/UX within the wallet app.

## 1 Opening & Unboxing
This process was pretty futuristic. The packaging came in an air-sealed silver chrome packaging, almost like some sort of capsule or “back-to-the-future”-type of package. Upon opening the packaging, you have a traditional phone box, and this one came with the phone, a charger, a silicone case, a setup guide, and two pieces of paper to write down the 16-word seed phrase. The 16-word seed phrase is important because as you will notice in the pictures, the phone has an in-built Seed Vault which allows the phone to act as an on-chain address/wallet, a naming service username, and a pseudo-Proof-of-Humanity.

## 2 Set-Up & Log-In
This process included the largest point of friction, but was a relatively intuitive process for a phone setup. There is an argument to be made for a crypto phone to have a web3-native OS and be natively embedded with “everything crypto”. However, I assume that this would mean a large departure from what people are already familiar when interacting with an iOS or Android phone. I think Solana Seeker’s decision to utilize a base-level Google Android OS (with all Google downloaded apps and interface) is actually very smart, and then they’ve embedded a Wallet app and a dApp store linked to the hardware wallet that is the phone. This lowers the barrier for new users to adapt this “crypto” phone as their primary one. Based on this, the login process was pretty easy as it was about setting up a normal Android phone, and I was able to transfer my iPhone data (existing apps, Google accounts, etc.) to this phone.

The hardest part was the seed phrase. After setting up the normal phone, I had to write down and securely store the 16-word seed phrase, and then undergo a 4-question test asking me about random parts of the phrase. To a crypto-native user, this is an assumption that people have gotten used to. For a regular user that wants to explore making this their primary phone (which is very possible given the low-friction integration of Solana crypto with a regular Android phone), this is the largest point of friction, and most will not understand the need for something like this. Improvement #1: Methods in which this could be eliminated. Maybe there is a “hidden” seed phrase connected to various layers of passkeys and authentication devices users are more used to setting up (facial recognition, fingerprint, PIN number, recovery alert, etc.).

## 3 Features
- Phone as an “everything” device: As I mentioned, I think the familiar Android OS + the crypto wallet and dApp store is a very smart decision. By lowering the barrier to entry, it makes it easier for folks to get used to having a “crypto” phone. So this phone, at a baseline, operates as a solid mid-range Android smartphone. In addition to this, it features a wallet connected to the Seed Phrase stored in the hardware enclave in the phone. So then, your phone becomes your onchain identity, is a Proof-of-Humanity (somewhat), becomes a device for distribution for any Solana project, and is self-funding because of exclusive airdrops.

- Venue for interaction: Natural digital work overtime slowly transitioned from all desktops to a mix of researching and executing on BOTH desktops and on a mobile device. This may happen in crypto too (think meme coin trading, native staking, etc.), and mobile app success is vital for apps. Solana makes this discovery, distribution, and interoperability much easier for its ecosystem projects through the phone and embedded wallet.

- MediocreUI/UX and Lack of Asset Diversity: The interface of the wallet itself is pretty mediocre, and I wouldn’t see myself trading assets such as crypto tokens and tokenized stocks within the wallet. In fact, there is also a lack of assets available within the wallet, and I found myself consistently downloading apps for different types of financial assets I wanted to trade (Drift for experimenting with perps, Jupiter for borrow/lend, etc.). While folks may continue to do this naturally due to their affinity to these platforms, new users may benefit from all these financial products being integrated directly into the Wallet app. A better interface for accessing all of this in one super-app for Solana finance would be cool.

Overall, this phone does an incredible job of blurring the lines between a normal phone and a “crypto” phone. I’m very excited about where this goes from here!
