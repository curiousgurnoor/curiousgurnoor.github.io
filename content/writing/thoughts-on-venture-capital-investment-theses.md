## How does one form an investment thesis?
I believe that the strongest components of an investment thesis are historical intuition, an expressed ”Why now?” demand, and a set of aligned incentives that accrue value to the right players. These factors inform which investment approach to take.

There are roughly two times to make a great investment. The **ideal path** is to catch the primitive early with high conviction. This does imply higher risk, but is offset with higher potential reward. The **secondary approach** is to let the initial run happen, wait for the consolidation and re-composition to occur, and then invest when the dust settles and valuations are favorable. This does imply less upside, but at this point, choosing a winner becomes slightly more intuitive. This is representative of the _Crypto J-Curve_ , where you time your investment based on the sustainable growth of a project’s core fundamentals and base-level value (Current Utility Value), and when there is minimal speculative value (Discounted Expected Utility Value) to allow for a favorable investment valuation.

The way to play a growing and consolidating market is to invest heavily into the consolidating incumbents _and_ to invest progressively in the insurgent platforms that will grow to commoditize incumbent business models and create a new wave of innovation.

History has a tendency to rhyme, and there are similar components that reflect within successive technological revolutions. See _The Investment Philosophy I’ve Gained at Placeholder_ . Over the long term, the user eventually absorbs pricing power, especially in verticals in which they are used to large points of friction and value extraction by middlemen. Incentives dictate the relative power balance between the supplier and the consumer, and an investment should be dictated by where the most value accrues.

Timing the market is vital. We need to ensure that the technology required to solve an expressed problem exists. Building a product without demand, or chasing after a problem which is too early to solve constitute potentially interesting, but tough-to-back projects.

## What verticals are currently interesting?
Fundamentally, people overlook the value of a basic unit/data/asset being composable, interactive, and free. That is why I strongly believe in decentralized infrastructure as compared to centralized counterparts that lead to closed-off environments, a lack of composability, and forced user lock-in. Decentralized infrastructure enables the power of composability and freedom, and this can be applied to a couple of innovative verticals.

**Collision of TradFi and Cryptonetworks** is quite interesting, as we will inevitably transition from archaic financial processes towards free and composable financial tokens that natively exist and are transacted onchain. We’re progressing in the right direction with current tokenization efforts.

**Decentralized Intelligence & Memory** will be the next iteration of user data, and will require the same decentralizing forces to break down high barriers of entry in the current Internet business model, and the future AI industry. Again, see _The Investment Philosophy I’ve Gained at Placeholder_ .

**New Open Standards in Hardware Innovation** will be quite interesting, as pick and shovels will always be vital underlying infrastructure for any data-intensive technology primitives. This is similar to the Fat Protocol Thesis and value accrual between the Layer 1 blockchain and an application built on top. Hardware investments may not have the same returns over time as compared to the viral growth of applications built on top, but hardware picks and shovels that support a completely new primitive are quite exciting, and necessary.

Lastly, **the funniest outcome is usually the most probable** , so investing in projects created by founders who have a compulsive or obsessive need to solve cool problems are pretty cool and intellectually engaging to support.

## The Investment Philosophy I’ve Gained at Placeholder
Technological cycles evolve in multi-year cycles of expansion, consolidation, and subsequent decentralization. A new open platform reduces the production costs of existing technology, leading to expansion, and the open platform becomes a shared standard to build interesting businesses on. Lower costs bring in new players and decentralize existing market powers. Lower prices imply more customers, larger markets, and lead to mass consumer applications. Successful businesses build on top of the open platform, leading to aggressive vertical/horizontal integrations. Suddenly, competition becomes tough, venture returns compress, and creating new value becomes tough. The cycle repeats as there is now a demand for a new low-cost open-source alternative standard.

Naturally, **entrepreneurial talent only moves to new markets where costs are low, competition is scarce, and the upside is high.**

If we contextualize this historical pattern, the origin of innovation and new value accrual shifts upwards in the stack. Hardware, then Software, and now User Data. We’ve created new open platforms and decentralize the former two, and hopefully, cryptonetworks decentralize the latter. What is left to decentralize? Hopefully its intelligence and memory, especially with the meteoric rise of AI foundation models.

Currently, cryptonetworks collapse the cost of building and scaling information networks. The thesis, then, is that the **decentralization and standardization of the internet’s data layer is collapsing the production costs of information networks, eliminating data monopolies and creating new innovation.**

## What qualities do you look for when investing in founders?
- Intellectual curiosity towards their project and the right motivations to build

- Deep understanding of the technical constraints of the system they aspire to redesign

- Crisp internal logic of the problem space they want to solve and the worldview they hope to implement, but Mr. Market deems it currently undervalued

- Strong understanding of incentives, why their product will work, why there is an existential demand for the product, and ensuring that they aren’t building in a void
